Inside the book

Meet Joseph Smith through his heart.

The Heart of Joseph is a revealing portrait — intimate and documented — of the heart, character, and soul of Joseph Smith. Across five sections and twenty-three chapters, each uncovering a distinct aspect of his character, readers discover not simply what Joseph taught, but who he was — and how the way he lived became its own kind of teaching.

Those who read carefully may find themselves, almost unexpectedly, seeing the hand of God and the grace of Jesus Christ woven through this remarkable man.

Joseph Smith: Five

From the visible man to the covenant heart

Organized from the external to the internal, the five parts follow Joseph from physical presence and personal gifts toward character, relationship, moral life, and finally his higher eternal calling.

I

Physical & Spiritual Gifts

His commanding presence, indomitable strength, abilities, and gifts.

II

Temperament & Emotional Character

His joy, playfulness, resolve, vulnerability, and response to opposition.

III

Relational & Social Dimensions

His friendships, family bonds, loyalty, affection, and life with Emma.

IV

Moral Character & Attributes

His generosity, mercy, charity, courage, and willingness to give of himself.

V

Spiritual Capacity & Prophetic Gifts

His revelations, prophetic calling, spiritual power, and eternal purpose.

How the portrait is built

Two voices bring Joseph into the room.

The first voice

Those who knew him

Firsthand accounts, reminiscences, and observations surround the narrative like remembered voices at the edge of the room — allowing friends, family, associates, and observers to describe the Joseph they actually encountered.

The second voice

Joseph himself

His own declarations, candid journal entries, unguarded phrases, and every surviving letter he wrote to Emma open windows into his tenderness, burdens, loyalty, faith, and the covenant that held when distance and danger pressed hardest.

“Physically, intellectually, emotionally, and spiritually — a living human multitude.”

— Truman G. Madsen

Readers meet a man who challenged ministers to jumping contests after besting them in scripture, who stooped in a muddy street to clean the shoes of two frightened children, who wept in prayer for those who had turned against him, and who gave of himself — coat, cane, time, and ultimately life — without reservation.

This is history at human distance: close enough to hear the remembered voices, read Joseph's own words, and consider the heart behind them.

Who is this book for?

The believing reader. The curious reader. The honest skeptic.

For anyone who wants to encounter Joseph as a complete person rather than a flattened icon or a catalogued controversy. The text is supported by notes and references in Chicago style, but written to be read — not merely consulted.
